## Article abstract — scenario version

Industrial process emissions are a structurally significant component of greenhouse-gas inventories in resource-intensive transition economies, yet their representation in integrated assessment models remains coarse. This study characterises Kazakhstan’s Industrial Processes and Product Use (IPPU) emissions for 1990–2023, evaluates the comparability of inventory activity data and production statistics, and develops exploratory 2023–2050 process-emissions pathways. Historical emissions from the UNFCCC Common Reporting Tables (CRT 2025) were compared with CRF 2023 and Bureau of National Statistics data. Total IPPU emissions declined from 22,741 kt CO2eq in 1990 to 12,258 kt CO2eq in 1996, before recovering to 26,773 kt CO2eq in 2023. Metal industry accounted for 50.4% of 2023 IPPU emissions, mineral industry for 36.0%, and F-gases for 10.2%. CRT–CRF CO2eq differences require cautious interpretation because the submissions use AR5 and AR4 global-warming potentials. Process-level checks show exact agreement for 2021 clinker and ferroalloy output, whereas apparent Portland-cement and zinc differences arise from non-equivalent product definitions and boundaries. A 2023-calibrated, LEAP-ready process-emissions boundary was then applied to a flat baseline and two exploratory mitigation sensitivities. Included emissions remain at 23,552 kt CO2eq in the baseline, decline to 20,715 and 17,182 kt CO2eq in 2030 under moderate and ambitious mitigation, and reach 14,827 and 7,761 kt CO2eq in 2050, respectively. The scenarios exclude F-gases, aluminium PFCs and industrial fuel combustion; they are sensitivity pathways rather than forecasts. Transparent boundary definition and activity-data harmonisation are therefore prerequisites for credible bottom-up IPPU modelling.
