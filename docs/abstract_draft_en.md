## Revised short abstract

Industrial process emissions are a structurally significant component of greenhouse-gas inventories in resource-intensive transition economies, while their representation in global integrated assessment models remains coarse. This study characterises Kazakhstan’s Industrial Processes and Product Use (IPPU) emissions for 1990–2023 and examines the comparability of activity data underpinning national inventory reporting, as a foundation for subsequent LEAP-based scenario modelling. Historical IPPU emissions from the UNFCCC Common Reporting Tables (CRT 2025) were compiled and compared with prior reporting (CRF 2023) and production statistics from the Bureau of National Statistics (BNS). Total IPPU emissions declined from 22,741 kt CO2eq in 1990 to 12,258 kt CO2eq in 1996, then recovered to 26,773 kt CO2eq in 2023. Metal industry remained the largest source in 2023 (50.4%), followed by mineral industry (36.0%) and F-gases (10.2%). In overlapping years, CRT 2025 totals were 0.118% lower than CRF 2023 in 2020 and 1.958% lower in 2021; these CO2eq differences must be interpreted cautiously because the submissions use AR5 and AR4 global-warming potentials, respectively. Activity-data checks show exact agreement for 2021 clinker and ferroalloy production, while apparent differences for Portland cement and zinc arise from non-equivalent product definitions and process boundaries. The findings demonstrate that transparent harmonisation of activity data is essential before using bottom-up inputs in future LEAP projections.
